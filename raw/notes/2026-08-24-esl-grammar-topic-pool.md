# ESL Grammar Topic Pool — Ranked by Search Demand, Error Frequency & Teaching Attention

**Compiled:** 2026-08-24
**Method:** Triangulation of three independent research streams —
1. **Search demand** (SpyFu US keyword volumes, Google Trends regions/rising queries, ELL Stack Exchange "Frequent" tab, ESL site traffic data)
2. **Learner-error frequency** (NUCLE, Cambridge Learner Corpus, TOEFL11, ICLE, NICT JLE corpora + error-analysis studies)
3. **Teaching attention** (unit counts in *English Grammar in Use* / Azar, British Council LearnEnglish lesson engagement, engVid/YouTube view counts)

**Reading the tiers:**
- 🟥 **Tier 1 (S)** — confirmed by all three streams; the highest-demand ESL grammar problems worldwide
- 🟧 **Tier 2 (A)** — confirmed by two streams, or one stream with very strong evidence
- 🟨 **Tier 3 (B)** — consistently popular, moderate demand; level- or L1-specific spikes

> Caveat: keyword volumes are US-market figures; global demand (especially India, Pakistan, the Philippines) is typically several times higher. Volumes below are quoted from sources, not invented.

## 🟥 TIER 1 — Highest-demand topics (the "Big Eight")

### 1. Tense system as a whole ("12 tenses", tense charts, how many tenses)
- **Search:** "tense/tenses/english tenses" cluster >35K/mo US (SpyFu); tense sites (englishpage.com) get ~120K visits/mo, top audience India
- **Errors:** tense/aspect ≈ 22–28% of all errors across multiple corpora
- **Teaching:** ~25% of Murphy's 145 units; every Azar level opens with tenses; top YouTube grammar category
- **Level:** A1→C1 (spiral curriculum)

### 2. Present perfect — especially *present perfect vs. past simple*
- **Search:** "present perfect" 30.1K/mo + "present perfect tense" 22K/mo US; canonical ELL post 176K views; "one of the most searched grammar topics for IELTS learners"
- **Errors:** "I have seen him yesterday" — most difficult tense; overused by French/Romance L1s, misused by Arabic L1s
- **Teaching:** 10 Murphy units; engVid present-perfect video 342K views
- **Level:** A2→B2 · **Hot regions:** Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, Poland, Italy

### 3. Articles — a / an / the / zero article
- **Search:** "An hour or a hour?" 340K views on ELL; "articles a an the rules" +130% rising on Trends
- **Errors:** ~12–21% of all ESL errors; 1 in 8 noun phrases for Chinese/Japanese/Russian learners; persists to C2
- **Teaching:** British Council articles lessons 319+222 comments; called "the single biggest grammatical pain for L2 learners"
- **Level:** A1→C1 · **Hardest for:** Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Russian, Slavic, Turkish, Thai, Urdu L1s

### 4. Prepositions — in/on/at; verb & adjective + preposition
- **Search:** "prepositions" 37.3K/mo US; JamesESL prepositions video = **1.89M views** (top engVid grammar video); "8 types of prepositions" +1250% rising
- **Errors:** Grammarly: wrong preposition is the **#1 most frequent mistake** overall; 17–29% of errors across corpora
- **Teaching:** 16 Murphy units; BC "Adjectives + prepositions" 625 comments (top of A1–A2 section)
- **Level:** A1→C1; substitution errors dominate at B2

### 5. Subject–verb agreement & 3rd-person -s
- **Search:** "subject verb agreement" 38.1K/mo US; "SVA class 10" +350% (Indian curriculum signal)
- **Errors:** top error in UAE/Malaysian studies; Chinese graduates omitted -s in ~50% of obligatory contexts — fossilization
- **Teaching:** Tier-2 in books but a constant re-teach at A1–B2
- **Level:** A1→B2 · **Hardest for:** Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Thai L1s

### 6. Conditionals (zero → third → mixed; "if clauses")
- **Search:** "conditional" 22.8K/mo US; engVid conditionals lessons in top lists
- **Errors:** "If I will go…"; counterfactuals most error-prone; universal at B1–C1
- **Teaching:** BC conditionals lessons 420 + 471 comments; flagship topic on Perfect English Grammar
- **Level:** A2→C1

### 7. Passive voice (active vs. passive)
- **Search:** "passive voice" 88.1K/mo US; "active vs passive" 19.9K/mo
- **Errors:** missing *be* ("the house built in 1990") — 55% of passive errors are omissions (Indonesian study)
- **Teaching:** dedicated Azar chapter; BC passive lessons 207+127 comments; CAE staple
- **Level:** B1→C1

### 8. Modal verbs (can/could/should/must/might; "modals")
- **Search:** persistent mid-volume; embedded in every ESL keyword universe
- **Errors:** "She can to swim", "I will can go" — wrong modal/auxiliary = 27.6% of verb errors (Malaysian study)
- **Teaching:** 12 Murphy units; 3 Azar chapters; flagship category on major sites
- **Level:** A1→C1

## Cross-cutting insights for using this pool

1. **The stable "Big Five" of learner pain** (articles, prepositions, tense/aspect, SVA, noun countability) accounts for the majority of errors in every corpus surveyed — all five are Tier 1/2 here.
2. **Morphology first, lexico-grammar later:** low-level demand clusters on forms (verb endings, -s, irregular verbs); advanced demand shifts to collocation, register, participle clauses, inversion.
3. **L1 reshuffles but never removes:**
   - Article-less L1s (CJK, Slavic, Turkish, Thai) → 3–4× more article errors
   - Arabic L1 → copula omission, double negatives, perfect-tense misuse
   - Romance L1s → perfect-tense overuse, preposition transfer
   - Chinese L1 → adverb placement, subject omission, relative-clause resumption
4. **India/Pakistan drive curriculum-shaped search spikes** ("reported speech class 9", "SVA class 10", "…examples in hindi/urdu") — high-value angle for content targeting.
5. **Exam-aligned topics** (conditionals, inversion, participle clauses, passives for IELTS/CAE) spike at B2–C1.
