> search demand — SpyFu US keyword volumes, Google Trends, ELL Stack
> Exchange "Frequent" tab, ESL site traffic; (2) learner-error frequency —
> NUCLE, Cambridge Learner Corpus, TOEFL11, ICLE, NICT JLE corpora +
> error-analysis studies; (3) teaching attention — unit counts in *English

## 🟥 Tier 1 — "The Big Eight" (confirmed by all three streams)
1. **Tense system as a whole** ("12 tenses", tense charts) — tense cluster >35K/mo US search (SpyFu); tense/aspect ≈22–28% of all corpus errors; ~25% of Murphy's 145 units. Level A1→C1.
2. **Present perfect** (esp. vs. past simple) — "present perfect" 30.1K/mo + "present perfect tense" 22K/mo US; 10 Murphy units. Level A2→B2. Hot regions: Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, Poland, Italy.
3. **Articles** (a/an/the/zero) — "articles a an the rules" +130% rising (Trends); ~12–21% of all ESL errors, persists to C2. Level A1→C1. Hardest for Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Russian, Slavic, Turkish, Thai, Urdu L1s.
4. **Prepositions** (in/on/at; verb/adjective + preposition) — "prepositions" 37.3K/mo US; wrong preposition is Grammarly's #1 most frequent mistake overall (17–29% of errors across corpora). 16 Murphy units.
5. **Subject–verb agreement & 3rd-person -s** — "subject verb agreement" 38.1K/mo US; Chinese-L1 graduates omitted -s in ~50% of obligatory contexts (fossilization). Level A1→B2.
6. **Conditionals** (zero→third→mixed) — "conditional" 22.8K/mo US; counterfactuals most error-prone. Level A2→C1.
7. **Passive voice** — "passive voice" 88.1K/mo US; missing *be* is 55% of passive errors (Indonesian study). Level B1→C1.
8. **Modal verbs** (can/could/should/must/might) — wrong modal/auxiliary = 27.6% of verb errors (Malaysian study); 12 Murphy units, 3 Azar chapters. Level A1→C1.

## Cross-Cutting Insights (from the source)
1. The "stable Big Five" of learner pain — articles, prepositions, tense/aspect, SVA, noun countability — accounts for the majority of errors in every corpus surveyed; all five are Tier 1/2.
2. Morphology first, lexico-grammar later: low-level demand clusters on verb endings/-s/irregulars; advanced demand shifts to collocation, register, participle clauses, inversion.
3. L1 reshuffles but never removes the pattern: article-less L1s (CJK, Slavic, Turkish, Thai) → 3–4× more article errors; Arabic L1 → copula omission, double negatives, perfect-tense misuse; Romance L1s → perfect-tense overuse, preposition transfer; Chinese L1 → adverb placement, subject omission, relative-clause resumption.
4. India/Pakistan drive curriculum-shaped search spikes ("reported speech class 9", "SVA class 10", "...examples in hindi/urdu") — a high-value content-targeting signal.
5. Exam-aligned topics (conditionals, inversion, participle clauses, passives for IELTS/CAE) spike at B2–C1.
