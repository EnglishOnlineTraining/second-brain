- ## One-Line Essence
	- A triangulated ranking (search demand + learner-error corpora + teaching-material attention) of ESL grammar topics, with tense/aspect, present perfect, articles, prepositions, subject–verb agreement, conditionals, passive voice, and modals as the confirmed Tier 1 "Big Eight."
- ## Key Arguments
	- 1. Three independent evidence streams converge on the same handful of topics — that convergence is the whole point of the tiering, and is what makes Tier 1 meaningfully different from "someone's opinion of what's important."
	- 2. Error patterns are stable across the board (articles/prepositions/tense-aspect/SVA/countability) but get reshuffled by L1 — the same underlying difficulty surfaces differently depending on a learner's first language.
	- 3. Search-demand data carries a real, usable content-targeting signal beyond raw popularity: specific curriculum-driven spikes (India/Pakistan school-syllabus terms) point to exactly which audiences are searching for which topics.

- ## Evidence Quality
	- **Moderate-to-strong, with a caveat.** Grounded in real, named academic corpora and studies (NUCLE, Cambridge Learner Corpus, TOEFL11, ICLE, NICT JLE; Rozovskaya & Roth 2010 and others) plus independent commercial keyword-research tools (SpyFu, Google Trends) — not vendor self-reporting, unlike the two prior ingests. But it's a **secondary, compiled** source: none of the individual figures have been independently re-verified in this ingest, and the document is explicit about its own US-market search-volume bias.
